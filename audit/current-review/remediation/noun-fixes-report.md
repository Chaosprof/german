## Validation rejects excluded

- nouns_009 — Ernst: `austerity` is an established contextual translation of *Ernst* (for example, “austerity of style”), so the row does not teach a false sense. [Langenscheidt records this equivalence.](https://de.langenscheidt.com/englisch-deutsch/austerity)
- nouns_009 — Staatsbürger: `subject` is a valid context-labelled translation for a *Staatsbürger* of a monarchy; the row already leads with `citizen/national`. [Langenscheidt explicitly records this use.](https://de.langenscheidt.com/deutsch-englisch/staatsbuerger)
- nouns_013 — Atemluft: `Breathing air` and `respiratory air` are established technical English expressions; `air breathed in/breathable air` may be plainer but is not a necessary correction.
- nouns_013 — Klimaschutzmaßnahme: `Climate protection measure` is established policy English and accurately transparent; `climate-action/mitigation measure` may be stylistically preferable in a specific context, not universally corrective.
- nouns_016 — Frühgeburt: *Frühgeburt* has both dictionary senses: premature birth and a prematurely born baby/child. [Duden lists both.](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Fruehgeburt)
- nouns_017 — Tierschützer: major bilingual dictionaries accept `animal rights activist/campaigner` for *Tierschützer*; the welfare/rights distinction may merit a usage note but does not invalidate the gloss. [Dictionary evidence.](https://www.dict.cc/deutsch-englisch/Tiersch%C3%BCtzer.html)
- nouns_018 — Schlichtung: `arbitration` is an established bilingual-dictionary equivalent of *Schlichtung*, alongside conciliation, mediation and settlement. [Cambridge records both the narrower and broader equivalents.](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/german-english/schlichtung)
- nouns_018 — Translation: Duden explicitly records the learned/technical sense `Übertragung, Übersetzung`; `translation` is therefore correct, though domain labels would help. [Duden.](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Translation)
- nouns_021 — Hufe: *Hufe* is 7–15 hectares; British `oxgang` varied but was often around 13–20 acres, so it is at least as defensible as `hide`, often much larger. The proposed replacement is not reliably better. [Duden on Hufe](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Hufe); [University of York historical dictionary on oxgang](https://yorkshiredictionary.york.ac.uk/words/oxgang).
- nouns_022 — Klingeln: *Das Klingeln* is a valid and useful nominalised infinitive (`das Klingeln an der Tür`); productive formation alone does not make it noun-bank junk.
- nouns_025 — Softwaretest: German Softwaretest can denote an individual test or the testing process; software testing is a valid contextual equivalent.
- nouns_026 — Grundton: `keynote` is an established musical translation of *Grundton* (tonic/fundamental note), not a corpus error. [Cambridge dictionary.](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/german-english/grundton)
- nouns_030 — Flößerei: `Log driving` is an established English name for transporting timber by river and is not demonstrably wrong; `timber rafting` can be added as a clarifier, not imposed as a replacement. Duden defines the word by reference to *Flößen*. [Duden.](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Floeszerei)
- nouns_036 — Schmerzempfinden: `Sensation of pain` is grammatical and can denote pain perception; `pain perception` is cleaner, but the existing gloss is not a substantive error.
